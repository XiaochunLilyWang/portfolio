**Key Points**

- Within 72 hours, Huawei, Xiaomi and Apple each launched a foldable flagship — the first time in the seven years since foldables appeared that all three have gone head to head at once.
- Xiaomi and Huawei both gave their newest in-house chips their debut in a foldable rather than in the higher-volume slab flagship.
- All three wide-fold inner screens land near a 1.41:1 ratio — the proportions of a sheet of A4 paper.
- Memory has gone from 10–15% of a phone's bill of materials to 30–40%, and foldables absorb that far more easily than mid- and low-end handsets.
- Counterpoint expects global smartphone shipments to fall 14.3% in 2026 while foldables grow 24% — yet foldable penetration will still be under 4% in 2027.

At 10 a.m. local time on 9 September, Apple held its autumn 2026 event at headquarters in Cupertino. After the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, AirPods 5, Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4 had each appeared, Apple's first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, finally took the stage.

It has a 5.4-inch outer screen and a 7.6-inch inner screen when opened, runs Apple's latest A20 Pro chip, and starts at $1,999 (15,999 yuan in China). Apple's new CEO John Ternus called it "the most transformative product since the original iPhone."

Apple was not the only company launching a foldable flagship that week. On 7 September, Xiaomi and Huawei released the Xiaomi 18 Fold and the tri-fold Mate XT 2, starting at 10,999 and 19,999 yuan respectively. Within three days, all three manufacturers had put a foldable at the centre of their launch event.

Another sign of the foldable's new centrality is where the in-house chips debuted. Convention would give the newest in-house silicon to the highest-volume slab flagship first. This time, Xiaomi's latest chip, the XRING O3, debuted in the Xiaomi 18 Fold, and Huawei's latest, the Kirin 9050 Pro, in the Mate XT 2 — which Huawei walked through in detail on stage, the first time it has done so since the Mate 40.

## All three foldable flagships are almost the same size

The iPhone Duo folds inward along its long edge. Closed, the user faces a 5.4-inch outer screen, like a closed book; opened, a 7.6-inch rounded inner screen with a matte nano-texture surface, intended to cut reflections and soften the crease.

Apple's iOS 27 has rearranged the interface for the larger screen. Lock-screen shortcuts, the home screen's persistent app bar, and in-app navigation bars and action buttons have all moved to the side of the screen, leaving more vertical space for content. Opened, the phone can run two apps side by side, or two windows of the same app. Third-party apps including Netflix, Slack and Zoom have already designed layouts for the new dimensions. Later this year the iPhone Duo will also support Apple Pencil.

Both the inner and outer screens have a 1.42:1 aspect ratio, so apps don't have to change layout drastically as the phone opens and closes, and the image carries over fairly naturally between the two screens. Notably, the iPhone Duo's size and proportions are nearly identical to Xiaomi's new 18 Fold and Huawei's Pura X Max from April — but it loses on thickness and weight.

## Seven years in, foldables have arrived at the proportions of A4 paper

1.41:1 is close to √2:1, the aspect ratio of A4 paper. On a foldable phone this corresponds to the "wide fold" form, between the "small fold" of a slab phone folded top to bottom and the "large fold" of two slab phones joined side by side. Xiaomi calls this form the "medium fold"; Apple hasn't named it.

The three manufacturers reached this ratio by different routes.

Huawei released its first large-fold phone, the Mate X, in February 2019, and has since tried outward folds, inward folds, vertical small folds, wide folds and tri-folds — nearly every major form that can be mass-produced. Its first wide fold, the Pura X, used 16:10, and Richard Yu wrote a piece at the time explaining the choice; a year later the Pura X Max switched to 1.41:1.

Xiaomi began with large folds in 2021 with the MIX Fold, and later added the MIX Flip small fold. This year it skipped the name "MIX Fold 5," calling the new phone the Xiaomi 18 Fold and folding it from the exploratory MIX line into the numbered flagship series.

Apple's work on foldables actually started earlier. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman has said Apple began developing foldable devices as early as 2016, but never settled on a form; development only accelerated after 2020. By the iPhone Duo's launch in 2026, the form had converged.

## Rising memory prices have, paradoxically, given foldables an opening

High prices used to be the main reason foldables failed to spread. Now that has become an advantage.

The global smartphone market has stopped growing. Data from Counterpoint shows annual global smartphone shipments falling from nearly 1.4 billion in 2021 to a little over 1.2 billion in 2025. Foldables are one of the few categories still growing: over the same period shipments rose from under 10 million to close to 20 million, and penetration from about 0.7% to 1.6%.

The divergence widened this year. In an August 2026 update, Counterpoint put the full-year decline in global smartphone shipments at -14.3%, the largest annual drop on record — a forecast successively revised down from -2.1% last December. The same forecast has foldables growing 24% this year to nearly 25 million units, and to around 33 million in 2027, when penetration would rise above 3%.

What opened the gap is memory. Demand for memory chips from AI servers has squeezed the DRAM and NAND supply available to phones. TrendForce data shows memory used to account for 10–15% of a phone's bill of materials, and had risen to 30–40% in 2026. Xiaomi president Lu Weibing has said the cost of a 12GB plus 512GB memory configuration rose by roughly 1,500 yuan compared with the first quarter of 2025. For a phone selling at three or four thousand yuan, that added cost is very hard to pass on to consumers in full. Manufacturers either raise prices or cut memory and storage, and both choices can hurt sales.

Foldables face a different set of consumers who have already accepted their premium positioning, so there is more room to absorb a price rise. With flexible displays, hinges and more complex construction, they already sell for over 10,000 yuan. The same added 1,500 yuan is a third or even half the price of a three-to-four-thousand-yuan slab phone; on a foldable selling for 10,000 to 20,000 yuan, the share is far smaller.

Apple's entry may push the market higher still. Counterpoint expects the iPhone Duo's 2026 shipments to be capped at around 6 million by early capacity constraints, yet still to take 25% of the global foldable market. By 2027, as supply increases, Apple's share could reach 40%.

Foldables will not, of course, become a mass product overnight. Even on the most optimistic forecast, by 2027 they are fewer than 4 in every 100 phones. Their more realistic role is as the premium flagship where manufacturers can house expensive components and new technology.

## Phone makers don't want to hand over the AI entry point

Opening Apple's event, Ternus didn't introduce a product first. He described an ideal AI device: one that is always with the user and understands personal context; that has a camera and microphone and can perceive its surroundings; that can run models locally and also call on cloud compute; and that has a large enough screen and battery. Apple calls this class of device an "intelligent personal hub," and argues the iPhone remains best suited to the role.

The framing reveals an anxiety shared by phone makers in the AI era: competing with model companies for the user's entry point.

In 2025 OpenAI merged with io, the hardware company founded by former Apple chief design officer Jony Ive, and began developing its own AI device. The same year ByteDance launched the "Doubao phone," whose built-in assistant can act across apps on the user's behalf once instructed — though it was quickly blocked en masse by the major apps; the latest reports say a second-generation Doubao phone will launch on 16 September. What model companies want is not only to put AI inside existing devices, but to redefine which device people use AI through.

The phone makers' chip is system-level permission. The phone is always with the user, has camera, microphone and location access, and holds photos, calendars and email. Compared with a third-party AI product, AI inside the operating system can understand more personal information, obtain more complete access, and has a better chance of completing complex tasks across apps.

## On-device AI is hardware-hungry, and foldables can afford it

But getting AI genuinely into the phone requires on-device inference. Photos, contacts and real-time location are not suited to being uploaded wholesale to servers for privacy and security reasons; voice and screen understanding need fast responses; and given the cost of token compute, running models locally and offline is the inevitable choice.

Apple, Xiaomi and Huawei have all taken the hybrid on-device-plus-cloud route, and all use the same approach on the device: large total parameters, with only a small portion activated at a time. Apple's third-generation Apple Foundation Models keep the full 20-billion-parameter model in flash storage, loading the needed portion into memory per task and activating only 1 to 4 billion parameters at a time. Huawei deployed a 30-billion-parameter Pangu model on the Mate XT 2 for the first time, activating 2 billion at a time and able to organise the local photo library offline. The Xiaomi 18 Fold carries the Xiaomi MiMo on-device model.

Even compressed, these models need more memory, higher bandwidth and stronger chips, and running them continuously drives up power draw and heat. A foldable's premium positioning absorbs that cost more easily, and the multiple body sections open up new layout space for battery and cooling — the iPhone Duo uses two batteries with an algorithm allocating energy between them, and the A20 Pro is attached to a custom vapour chamber.

## AI has increased the demand for a bigger screen

The foldable's other obvious advantage is screen size. The iPhone Duo allows two apps side by side; the Mate XT 2 supports three windows in parallel; the Xiaomi 18 Fold can run two or three windows. This underpins a judgement behind foldables: the stronger AI gets, the more information people may need to see, not less.

In the agent era, AI doesn't only generate answers — it acts on the user's behalf. When files are being modified, messages sent, payments made, or several tasks run at once, people generally need to know what the AI is doing, and to confirm or take over at key steps. What a foldable offers is an interface close to a tablet's, folded into a pocket, giving human-AI collaboration more room.

Judging by the demos at this event, the main capabilities of Apple's Siri AI are not much changed from the version announced in June: natural conversation, personal context understanding, screen awareness and cross-app actions. Apple has simply used these capabilities to build a new narrative around the "intelligent personal hub."

That narrative has an obvious gap in the Chinese market. Siri AI and the new generation of Apple Intelligence cannot yet launch in mainland China, and Chinese consumers may end up with an expensive large-screen foldable iPhone rather than the complete AI system Ternus described on stage. Facing Huawei and Xiaomi, which already have mature foldable products and local AI services, will be the iPhone Duo's most immediate test.
